# Five Distortions Reference

## Outside Distortions

### 1. Authority
- Halo effect from impressive people who lack domain expertise
- FOMO / urgency framing
- Veil of secrecy that resists basic questions
- Classic cases: Theranos, Enron, FTX, WeWork, 2008 crisis

Key question: What needs to be true for this to be real?

### 2. True Lies
Technically true but designed-to-mislead language:
- "Up to X"
- "As low as Y"
- "Starting at Z"
- "Clinically proven" / "recommended by experts" without numbers or conditions
- Impressive-sounding statements that convey almost no information

Key move: Restate the claim with a question mark and demand baselines.

### 3. Groupthink
- Asch conformity experiments: people deny clear evidence to match the group
- One dissenting voice is often enough to restore independent judgment
- Historical: Salem witch trials, tulip mania, bank runs, dot-com frenzy

Key move: Force the strongest dissenting case (use AI as devil's advocate).

## Internal / Modern Distortions

### 4. AI Outsourcing
- Reduced ownership and depth when relying on generative AI for thinking
- Fluency mistaken for correctness
- Inability to restate or defend the core logic in one's own words

Protocol: Be precise. Please verify. Cross-check. Require human restatement.

### 5. Self-Deception / Motivated Reasoning
- Wanting a story to be true causes selective blindness
- Emotional or identity investment blocks inconvenient facts

Key question: What am I refusing to see because I need this story to be true?

## Deepfake / Reality Context
In an environment where video, voice, and text can be perfectly faked, the only durable defense is trained individual judgment plus systematic questioning.
